# Self-check: is it correct, and how do others see it?

A statement can sound right and still be wrong; a page can serve one reader and miss another. This skill questions the result before the user spends time on it.

## When
- Before `review: needed` on a page that states goals, findings, recommendations, a method or a plan.
- After a substantial rewrite.
- Not for housekeeping (links, spelling, renaming).

## Step 1: Is it correct?
Answer each with yes, no or unclear, and one line of reason:
1. Evidence: does every factual statement rest on a checked source or on a stated assumption?
2. Purpose: does the page say what it is for and what decision it enables? For a goal: what for, and how will we know it is reached?
3. Fit: does every part serve that purpose? Does anything belong on another page?
4. Consistency: does it contradict another page, a decision record or the rules?
5. Fact or opinion: are assessments marked as such?
6. Blind spots: what would a sceptical reader ask first? Is it answered?

## Step 2: How do different readers see it?
For each reader: what they need, what they would object to, what is missing. Two lines at most. Default readers:

| Reader | Typical questions |
|---|---|
| The user in six months | Can I still follow this without the chat? Are dates, sources and reasons there? |
| A sceptical friend | Is this true? Where is the evidence? What is the cost? |
| Someone new to the topic | Are terms explained? Is the main point clear in the first lines? |

Replace or add readers when the page has a specific audience. These are perspectives to test the text, not real people's opinions; never invent what a real person thinks.

## Step 3: Trade-offs
Name where needs pull in different directions. State the trade-off; do not resolve it silently.

## Step 4: Act and report
- Fix what is clearly wrong and note it as fixed.
- Leave decisions to the user as open questions.
- Add or replace one callout at the end of the page:

```
> [!check] Self-check YYYY-MM-DD
> Correct: <what holds, what was fixed>
> Readers: <one line per reader where something is missing>
> Trade-offs: <one line>
> Open for the user: <questions>
```

- Ask the open questions one at a time, with a recommendation.
